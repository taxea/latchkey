## Why this is the first tool to set up

Reused passwords are how most accounts are taken over: one old breach, tried everywhere. A password manager makes every password unique without you remembering any of them, fills them in so you never type them into a fake page, and, in the family plans, lets a spouse or adult child get in if something happens to you.

## How we tested

Each manager was set up from scratch on an iPhone, an Android phone and a Windows laptop, then set up a second time for a parent in their seventies with the tester on the phone. We scored how easy the first week was, how well it handled a locked-out parent, whether family sharing and emergency access worked, the three-year cost, and what the company does with data.

## The picks

**1Password** is the pick for families: the cleanest apps, the best family sharing, passkey support everywhere, and a "travel mode" and emergency kit that make the recovery story clear. It costs more than the others.

**Bitwarden** is the runner-up and the best value: open source, free for one person, a cheap family plan, and emergency access built in. The apps are plainer, which some parents prefer.

**Apple Passwords and Google Password Manager** are free and good if the whole family is on one platform. They lack cross-platform sharing and true emergency access.

## What to skip

Any manager that cannot hand your vault to a named person after a waiting period. That feature is the difference between a tool and a plan.
