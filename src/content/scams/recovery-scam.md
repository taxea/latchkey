definition: The recovery scam targets people who were already scammed, with a call or message from a "lawyer," "recovery agency" or "government investigator" offering to get the lost money back for an upfront fee, which is itself the scam.

redFlags:
  - They contact you, and they already know you were scammed and how much you lost.
  - A fee, tax, or "processing charge" must be paid before the recovered funds are released.
  - Payment by gift card, wire or crypto, the same methods as the first scam.
  - A website full of testimonials and a "guarantee."
whatToDo:
  - Hang up. No government agency or legitimate firm charges a fee to recover scam losses, and none contacts victims cold.

  - Tell anyone in the family who was scammed that this call will come.

The cruelest scam in the database. Weeks or months after someone loses money, a call comes from an "investigator" who says the scammers have been caught and funds are being returned to victims. There is paperwork, a case number, sometimes a convincing website. There is also a fee: a tax on the recovered amount, a release charge, a lawyer's retainer. Pay it and a second fee appears, then a third.

The callers have the victim's name, the amount and the story because the first scammers sold the list. That is also why the family should know: the person who was scammed once is the person most likely to be called again.
