  - The caller asks you to read back a code that was just texted to you.

whatToDo:
  - Hang up. Call the number on the back of your card and ask whether the fraud team contacted you.

  - If a text alert seems real, respond only inside your bank's app, never to the text.

  - Change your online banking password and the password on your email.
  - If you read a code to them, assume they are in the account; have the bank lock it and reissue cards.

It starts with a text that looks exactly like a real fraud alert: "Chase: did you attempt a $912.47 purchase at Best Buy? Reply YES or NO." You reply NO. The phone rings, caller ID shows the bank, and a calm, professional voice says they are from the fraud department and will help you secure the account.

To "verify your identity," they send a code to your phone and ask you to read it back. That code is your bank's two-factor login; reading it lets them in. Or they explain that your account is compromised and you must move the balance to a "secure account" they set up, or send yourself a Zelle payment to "cancel" the fraudulent one. The Zelle goes to a phone number they control.

The whole script depends on you staying on the line. Hanging up and calling the bank yourself ends it every time.
