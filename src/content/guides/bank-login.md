---
title: Lock down your bank login
service: Bank login
category: banking
atRisk: Online banking is the account scammers are actually after; everything else is a route to it.
totalTime: PT10M
checklist:
  - A unique password from your password manager, never the one you use for email.
  - Two-factor with the bank's app or an authenticator, not text codes where the bank allows it.
  - Transaction alerts for every charge, and login alerts.
  - A verbal password or PIN on the phone banking line.
  - Your bank's real phone number saved in Contacts, from the back of your card.
steps:
  - name: Change the password
    text: Log in to the bank's website or app, go to Profile or Security settings, and set a long unique password saved in your password manager.
  - name: Turn on the strongest two-factor the bank offers
    text: In Security settings, look for two-factor, multi-factor or "extra security." Choose the bank's own app notification or an authenticator app if offered; text codes if that is all they have. Passkeys are arriving at most major banks; turn one on if you see the option.
  - name: Turn on alerts
    text: In Alerts or Notifications, turn on a text or push for every transaction above a small amount, every login from a new device, and every change to contact details. These are how you catch fraud in minutes instead of weeks.
  - name: Add a phone-banking password
    text: Call the bank (number on the back of your card) and ask to add a verbal password or PIN for phone banking. It stops someone with your personal details from calling in as you.
  - name: Save the real number
    text: Put the bank's phone number from the back of your card into Contacts as "Bank, real number." When a "fraud department" calls, you hang up and call this.
  - name: Review linked accounts and payees
    text: In Transfers or Zelle settings, look at linked external accounts and saved payees. Remove anything you do not recognize.
  - name: Check contact details
    text: Confirm the email and phone on the account are yours. A changed email is how scammers hide the confirmations.
forParent:
  - Turn on transaction alerts to your phone as well as theirs, if the bank allows a second contact, or set up view-only access through the bank's trusted-contact program.
  - "Save the real number in their Contacts and show them the rule: never call a number from a text, always the one on the card."
  - Ask the bank about a trusted contact or power of attorney on file, so you can act if needed.
recovery:
  - Call the number on the back of the card, say "fraud," and ask them to lock online access and review activity.
  - Change the email password too; the bank login reset goes there.
tools: [a password manager, your debit or credit card for the real phone number]
related: [gmail, iphone, android]
scams: [bank-impersonation-scam, tech-support-scam]
faq:
  - q: My bank only offers text-message codes. Is that safe?
    a: It is much better than no second factor. Protect it by adding a PIN and port-out lock at your carrier so your number cannot be moved to a scammer's phone.
  - q: Should I use Zelle at all?
    a: Zelle is fine for paying people you know. Treat it like cash; never send it to a stranger, a "fraud department" or yourself at a number someone gives you.

Every bank's settings look different, so this guide is organized by what to do rather than where to tap. The four most common banks (Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and Citi) all have each setting above; it may be called "security," "profile" or "alerts." If you cannot find one, call the real number and ask them to walk you through it.
