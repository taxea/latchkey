title: Lock down your iPhone
service: iPhone
category: phone
atRisk: Your phone is your bank, your email, your photos and your two-factor codes. A stolen passcode is a stolen life.
totalTime: PT10M
checklist:
  - Use a six-digit or alphanumeric passcode, never four digits, and never your birth year.
  - Turn on Stolen Device Protection.
  - Turn on Silence Unknown Callers and Filter Unknown Senders.
  - Turn on automatic updates.
  - Set Find My and make sure it is on.
steps:
  - name: Strengthen the passcode
    text: Settings, Face ID & Passcode, Change Passcode, then Passcode Options. Choose a six-digit code or a custom alphanumeric one. Not a birthday, not 123456.
  - name: Turn on Stolen Device Protection
    text: Settings, Face ID & Passcode, Stolen Device Protection. Turn it on. It requires Face ID (not the passcode) for sensitive changes when you are away from home, which stops the "shoulder-surfed passcode" theft.
  - name: Silence unknown callers
    text: Settings, Apps, Phone, Silence Unknown Callers. Calls from numbers not in your contacts go to voicemail. Real callers leave a message; scammers do not.
  - name: Filter unknown senders
    text: Settings, Apps, Messages, Filter Unknown Senders. Texts from unknown numbers land in a separate list with links disabled. Toll and delivery scams lose most of their power here.
  - name: Turn on automatic updates
    text: Settings, General, Software Update, Automatic Updates. Turn on everything. Updates close the holes scammers use.
  - name: Check Find My
    text: Settings, your name at the top, Find My, Find My iPhone. On, with Send Last Location on.
  - name: Lock the SIM with your carrier
    text: Call your carrier or use its app and add an account PIN and a port-out or number lock. This stops SIM swaps, where a scammer moves your number to their phone and receives your codes.
  - name: Review what has access
    text: Settings, Privacy & Security. Look at Location Services, Contacts and Photos; remove apps that have no reason to be there.
forParent:
  - Set the passcode to six digits they will remember; write it in their notebook, not on the phone case.
  - Turn on Silence Unknown Callers and explain that family is in Contacts, so family always gets through.
  - Add your Apple ID as the Legacy Contact and set up Family Sharing so you can locate the phone.
  - Set Emergency SOS and Medical ID while you are there.
recovery:
  - If the phone is lost, go to icloud.com/find from any device and mark it as lost; that locks it and shows a message.
  - If you forgot the passcode, you will need to erase the phone from icloud.com/find and restore from backup; make sure iCloud Backup is on today.

faq:
  - q: Will Silence Unknown Callers block my doctor's office?
    a: The call goes to voicemail and shows in Recents; they leave a message, you call back. Add the office to Contacts and it rings normally.
  - q: Is Face ID safe?
    a: Yes. It is far safer than a passcode because nobody can watch you type it. Stolen Device Protection makes it required for the changes that matter.

The most common iPhone theft now is not a pickpocket. It is someone watching you type your passcode in a bar, then taking the phone. With the passcode they can change your Apple ID password, turn off Find My, and open every banking app. Stolen Device Protection was built for exactly that, and most people have never turned it on.
