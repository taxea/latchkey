---
title: Lock down your Facebook account
service: Facebook
category: social
atRisk: A hacked Facebook account is used to scam your friends in your name, and it is the login for dozens of other apps.
totalTime: PT10M
checklist:
  - Change the password to a unique one from your password manager.
  - Turn on two-factor authentication with an authenticator app.
  - Review "Where you're logged in" and log out of everything you do not recognize.
  - Set three to five trusted contacts and a legacy contact.
  - Turn on login alerts.
steps:
  - name: Open the Accounts Center
    text: In the Facebook app, tap your profile picture, then Settings & privacy, Settings, and Accounts Center at the top. Choose Password and security.
  - name: Change the password
    text: Password and security, Change password. Use a long unique phrase saved in your password manager.
  - name: Turn on two-factor authentication
    text: Password and security, Two-factor authentication, choose your account, then Authentication app. Scan the code with your authenticator (or save it in your password manager). Save the recovery codes to your password manager's secure notes.
  - name: Review where you are logged in
    text: Password and security, Where you're logged in. Log out of any device or location you do not recognize.
  - name: Turn on login alerts
    text: Password and security, Login alerts. Turn on notifications and email so you hear about any new login immediately.
  - name: Set a legacy contact
    text: Settings, Accounts Center, Personal details, Account ownership and control, Memorialization. Choose who manages your account if something happens; it stops a dead person's account becoming scam bait.
  - name: Tighten who can see and find you
    text: Settings, Privacy, and go through "How people find and contact you." Limit friend requests to friends of friends and hide your email and phone from search.
forParent:
  - Do two-factor with the authenticator inside the password manager you set up for them, so the code is on the same device.
  - Set yourself as the legacy contact.
  - Limit friend requests to friends of friends; most "hacked friend" scams start with a fake duplicate profile.
  - "Teach the one rule: a friend asking for money or gift cards in Messenger is a hacked account; call them."
recovery:
  - Go to facebook.com/hacked from a device you have used before and follow the steps.
  - If the email on the account was changed, look for Facebook's "did you change your email" message in your inbox; it has a link to reverse it, valid for a limited time.
tools: [a password manager, an authenticator app]
related: [gmail, iphone, android]
scams: [grandparent-scam, recovery-scam]
faq:
  - q: Why does a hacked Facebook account matter so much?
    a: Because the scammer messages your friends as you, asking for money or sending links, and because many people used "Log in with Facebook" for other apps. Your account is a key to your friends' trust.
  - q: Someone made a duplicate profile of me. Is that hacking?
    a: "No, it is cloning: a new account with your name and photos. Report the profile as impersonation and tell your friends not to accept the request."

Facebook is where the family lives, which is why it is the platform scammers most want to be you on. A hacked account sends "I'm stuck and need $200" to everyone you know, and the people who answer are the ones who trust you most.
